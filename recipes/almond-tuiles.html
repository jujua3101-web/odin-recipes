Almond Tuile Cookies

Description

Super light, crispy, and baked until perfectly golden. These French-style almond cookies are surprisingly easy to make with just a few staple ingredients. They have a lovely buttery crunch and a sweet nutty flavor from toasted sliced almonds.

Ingredients

  • 2 egg whites
  • 50g - 80g confectioner sugar
  • 50g unsalted butter (melted)
  • 50g AP flour
  • 200g sliced almond
  • vanilla (optional)
  • 2g salt

Instructions

  1. Preheat your oven to 175°C and line a large baking sheet with parchment paper
  2. In a mixing bowl, whisk together the egg whites and granulated sugar just until combined and slightly frothy
  3. Whisk in the melted butter, vanilla extract, and a pinch of salt. Gently fold in the flour until the batter is smooth.
  4. Gently mix in the sliced almonds until evenly distributed.
  5. Pour the prepared almond tuile batter directly onto the center of the lined tray. Using an offset spatula or the back of a large spoon (or even hand), gently push and spread the batter outward from the center in an even, paper-thin layer across the pan.
  6. Bake for 8 to 10 minutes at 160°C to 170°C, or just until the outer edges are set and showing a light golden color, while the center remains pale and pliable.
  7. Take the tray out of the oven and use a sharp pizza cutter, bench scraper, or chef's knife to slice the warm sheet into rectangles, strips, or triangles.
  8. Return the tray to the oven for another 8 to 10 minutes until all the pieces are evenly golden-brown across the centers.
  9. Turn the oven temperature down to 50°C and switch on the oven fan. Wedge a wooden chopstick into the oven door to keep it cracked slightly open, letting excess steam escape. Leave the tuiles inside for 60 minutes.
  10. Remove the tuiles and let them come to room temperature.

Notes:

  • Sliced almond can be swapped for other nuts and seeds mix (sesame, sunflower seeds, hazelnut, etc.).
  • If the sheet hardens while you are cutting pop the pan back into the warm oven for 30 seconds to soften the batter up again.
  • Because of the 60-minute low-temp drying step, these will stay snap-crispy for up to 3 weeks stored at room temperature in a sealed container
  • Changing the AP:almond flour also changes the cookie texture. Increasing the amount of almond flour used while reducing AP flour (4:2 or 3:3) will result in more britle and delicate cookies.
